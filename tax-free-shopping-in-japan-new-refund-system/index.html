Tax-Free Shopping in Japan Since November 2026: How the New Refund System Works

Since November 1, 2026, tax-free shopping in Japan works differently. You no longer get the tax taken off at the counter. You pay the full price, including Japan’s 10% consumption tax (8% for some food), and you get the tax back later, after customs confirms at departure that you’re taking the goods out of Japan.

The refund is still worth having on big purchases, but there are a few new ways to lose it. This article walks through the whole process, from the shop to the airport to the money arriving.

The new system is still settling in, and refund methods differ by shop. When you buy, always ask the shop how its refund works.

What changed, in one paragraph

Under the old system, you showed your passport, paid the tax-free price and walked out. Under the new system, you show your passport and pay the tax-inclusive price. The shop records the purchase electronically against your passport. When you leave Japan, customs confirms the goods are leaving with you, and then the shop or its refund provider pays you back the tax by the method you agreed: to your card, as cash at a counter, by bank transfer or through an app.

So if a department store charges you full price after you’ve shown your passport, they haven’t made a mistake. That’s the new system.

Who can use it

Tax-free shopping is for short-term visitors, generally people who entered Japan as temporary visitors. People who live and work in Japan usually can’t use it. Japanese nationals living abroad may qualify with proof.

Two practical points:

  1. Carry your actual passport. A photo or a copy won’t work, because the shop needs to scan it.
  2. Check how you entered. Your passport should show a temporary visitor entry, either a landing sticker or an electronic record.

At the shop: the ¥5,000 rule

To qualify, you need to spend at least ¥5,000 before tax, at the same shop, on the same day.

Under the new rules, general goods (clothes, electronics, bags) and consumables (food, drinks, cosmetics, medicine) can be added together toward that ¥5,000. The old ¥500,000 upper limit on consumables has been removed. For very expensive single items, around ¥1,000,000 or more before tax, extra records may be required, so ask the shop.

What to do at the counter:

  1. Look for the “Tax-Free” sign.
  2. Pay the full price.
  3. Show your passport and make sure the purchase is registered as tax-free.
  4. Ask how the refund will be paid, and keep every receipt and document they give you.

Two common mistakes here: counting the tax-inclusive price toward ¥5,000 (it’s the price before tax that counts), and splitting purchases at the same shop across different days.

Don’t use what you bought

Tax-free goods are meant to leave Japan. Anything you use, eat or wear in Japan doesn’t qualify for the refund. The special sealed bags that used to be required are gone, but that makes it easier to slip up, not harder.

  • Don’t open tax-free cosmetics, eat tax-free snacks or wear tax-free clothes during your trip.
  • Keep items in their original packaging if you can.
  • Pack tax-free items together, apart from the things you’re using.
  • If you want to use something right away, buy it separately without tax-free.

The 90-day rule

Customs must confirm your goods within 90 days of purchase. If you’re in Japan for a long trip, anything you bought tax-free more than 90 days before you leave won’t qualify.

If you’re staying several months, do your tax-free shopping in your last three months, note the purchase dates, and take the goods out with you. Don’t post them home separately.

At the airport: before you check in

This is the step most visitors don’t know about yet.

  1. Arrive early. Leave extra time for the tax-free check.
  2. Before you check in your luggage, find a tax-free procedure terminal in the departure lobby and scan your passport. Some airports also let you use Visit Japan Web. Follow the airport’s own instructions.
  3. If the screen says you need an inspection (often shown in red), take your items to the customs counter.
  4. Only after that, check in your bags.

The order matters. If your tax-free purchases are already in a checked suitcase, you can’t show them to customs, and you may lose the refund.

Getting your money back

After customs confirms the export, the shop or its refund provider pays you back by the method you chose. How long it takes, and whether there’s a fee, depends on the shop or provider.

  1. Keep your receipts and refund documents until the money arrives. Don’t throw them away at the airport.
  2. Save the shop’s or provider’s contact details.
  3. If you chose a card refund, check your statement for a credit.
  4. If nothing arrives within the time the shop told you, contact them with your receipt and passport details.

When something goes wrong

Most problems come from four things: the purchase wasn’t registered properly, the 90 days passed, an item was used, or the goods were in checked luggage. Some of these can’t be fixed at the airport, so prevention matters most.

  • At each shop, check that your receipt shows the purchase as tax-free.
  • If the airport terminal can’t find a purchase, show your receipt to customs staff and ask there, calmly.
  • If one item doesn’t qualify, you lose the refund for that item only. Other items can still be refunded.

Five phrases for tax-free shopping

Japanese Romaji Meaning
免税でお願いします。 Menzei de onegai shimasu. Tax-free, please.
免税の対象ですか？ Menzei no taishō desu ka? Is this eligible for tax-free?
還付はどうやって受け取れますか？ Kanpu wa dō yatte uketoremasu ka? How do I get the refund?
免税の手続きはどこですか？ Menzei no tetsuzuki wa doko desu ka? Where do I do the tax-free procedure?
購入履歴が見つかりません。 Kōnyū rireki ga mitsukarimasen. The purchase record can’t be found.

The short version

  • Since November 2026: pay full price, get the tax back later.
  • Short-term visitors only. Bring your real passport.
  • ¥5,000 or more before tax, same shop, same day.
  • Don’t use tax-free items in Japan.
  • Leave Japan within 90 days of purchase.
  • Scan your passport at the airport before you check in your bags.
  • Keep your receipts until the refund arrives.

Rules and procedures can change, and each shop handles refunds its own way. If a shop, the airport or customs tells you something different from what’s here, follow what they say.
