Why Are Japanese Houses So Cold? How to Stay Warm for Less

Japanese houses feel cold in winter because most have no central heating, and many have thin walls and single-pane windows that lose heat fast. Japanese people don’t try to heat the whole home. They heat the room they’re in, usually with the air conditioner, and heat their body with a kotatsu, an electric blanket or warm layers. Once you do the same, your rooms get warmer and your bill gets smaller.

Electricity prices, rental rules and product prices change. Your rental contract, your heater’s manual and your landlord’s instructions come first. This is not medical advice.

Why it’s warmer outside than in your living room

It can be 10°C outside while you sit in a coat at home. There’s no radiator anywhere, only an air conditioner high on the wall. That’s normal in Japan. Heat escapes through the windows, so the first step costs almost nothing:

  • Close the curtains at dusk. Thick curtains that reach the floor work best.
  • Put a rug on bare flooring.
  • Close the doors to the rooms you’re not using, so you only heat one room.

Then pick your main heat. If your room has an air conditioner that heats, make it your main heat. If it doesn’t, check whether your contract allows kerosene or gas heaters. Many apartments don’t. If it doesn’t allow them, use electric heat close to your body.

The air conditioner remote, in Japanese

Every button is in Japanese, so many people press the wrong one. These are the words you need:

Japanese Reading English Notes
暖房 danbō Heating The one you want in winter
冷房 reibō Cooling Summer
除湿 joshitsu Dehumidify Not for heating
自動 jidō Auto Fan or mode on auto
運転 / 停止 unten / teishi Start / Stop Also 入 / 切 (on / off)
風向 kazamuki Air direction Point down in winter
霜取り shimotori Defrosting Warm air pauses for a while

Choose 暖房, set the fan to 自動 and point the vents down, because warm air rises. Japan’s Ministry of the Environment suggests a room temperature of around 20°C in winter, so start there.

If the warm air stops for a few minutes on a cold day, the unit is usually defrosting the outdoor part (霜取り). Wait, and it starts again. Also clean the filter: many makers suggest about every two weeks in heavy use, because a dusty filter wastes power. Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, bikes and boxes.

What each heater costs per hour

Your first winter electricity bill can be far higher than the autumn one. Heat that touches your body is usually the cheapest:

Heater Cost per hour (estimate)
Electric blanket, often 20–80 W (our estimate) About ¥1–¥3
Kotatsu (TEPCO estimate) About ¥2.5–¥5
Electric carpet, 2-mat size, 200–400 W (our estimate) About ¥6–¥12
Kerosene fan heater, including fuel (TEPCO estimate) About ¥10–¥35
Air conditioner (TEPCO estimate; range about ¥4–¥40) About ¥19.7
Ceramic or oil heater at 1,000 W (our estimate) About ¥31

These are estimates as of November 2026, not measurements. TEPCO’s figures (September 2026) assume ¥31 per kWh and kerosene at ¥140.8 a litre, and our estimates use the same ¥31. Your rate, model, room and the weather change the real cost.

To work out your own heater, check the watts on its label and multiply the kW by your rate per kWh. A 1,000 W heater is 1 kW, so at ¥31 per kWh it costs about ¥31 an hour.

The cheap pattern most Japanese homes use: run the air conditioner to warm the room, then lower it and sit at the kotatsu. A kotatsu is a low table with a heater underneath and a quilt over it. An evening of four hours at the kotatsu plus one hour of air conditioning comes to about ¥30–¥40.

Kerosene heaters: only if your contract allows it

A kerosene fan heater warms a room in minutes, but it burns fuel inside the room and can produce carbon monoxide. Check your contract and the heater manual first.

  • Use only kerosene (灯油, tōyu), never petrol (gasoline). Petrol in a kerosene heater can catch fire.
  • Japan’s product safety agency NITE advises opening a window for about 5 minutes at least once an hour.
  • Keep curtains, laundry and bedding away. The manual gives the distance.
  • Turn it off before bed and before you go out.

You can buy kerosene at petrol stations and some home centres, in a kerosene tank (ポリタンク). Some areas have delivery trucks.

Water on the windows and black spots on the curtains

Many people find water running down the inside of the windows every morning, then black mould on the rubber seals and the back of the curtains by January. Warm, damp air hits the cold glass and turns into water, and mould grows where water stays.

  1. Wipe the windows and frames each morning with a towel or a window squeegee (結露取りワイパー).
  2. Open two windows on opposite sides for 5 to 10 minutes a day so air moves through.
  3. Run the bathroom and kitchen fans while you cook and bathe, and for a while after.
  4. Keep furniture a few centimetres away from outside walls.
  5. Don’t run a humidifier at full power all day. Aim for about 40–50% humidity (the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism’s guidance) and check it with a cheap hygrometer.

If you leave a futon on the floor all week, mould can grow underneath. Fold it up or air it.

Warm the bathroom before you bathe

Stepping from a freezing bathroom into a very hot bath strains the body. In Japan this is often called “heat shock”. In 2019, 4,900 people aged 65 and over drowned in home baths in Japan.

Warm the bathroom first with the bathroom heater (浴室暖房) or shower steam. Keep the bath at 41°C or below and soak for 10 minutes or less (the Consumer Affairs Agency’s guide numbers). Skip the bath right after alcohol, a big meal or medicine, and stand up slowly.

Buying a heater: know your room size in mats

Heater labels in Japan give two room sizes in jō (mats): one for 木造 (wooden buildings) and one for コンクリート (concrete). Find your room size on your floor plan, and use the number for your building type. A heater rated for a 6-mat concrete room is too weak for a 6-mat wooden room.

If the labels are confusing, show this to staff at an electronics shop:

［　］畳の部屋です（木造／鉄筋コンクリート）。合うヒーターはどれですか？

My room is [ ] mats (wooden / concrete building). Which heater suits it?

When the heater breaks

If the air conditioner came with the apartment, repairs are often the landlord’s job. Before calling a repair company yourself, contact the management company. Write down the maker, the model number (on a sticker on the side) and any blinking light or error code, and attach a photo. This sentence says what’s wrong:

エアコンの暖房が効きません。修理の手配をお願いできますか？

The air conditioner isn’t heating. Could you arrange a repair?

Equipment listed as 設備 (setsubi) in your contract is usually the landlord’s to repair. Things a past tenant left behind (残置物) may not be.

A quick checklist

Heat yourself and the room you’re in, not the whole building. Small habits like closing the curtains at dusk and wiping the windows each morning save money and keep mould away.
